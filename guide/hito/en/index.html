The people whose stories are woven into Misaki — the poet who sang of rain on Jogashima, and more. Their traces are still here to visit.

People of the pastWriters, lords and others

PoetMisaki & Jogashima

Kitahara Hakushu1885–1942 · poet

In 1913 he moved to Misaki and lived here for about nine months, said to have stayed in a Western-style house at Mukogasaki and in temporary lodgings at Kentoji Temple. Here he wrote “Rain on Jogashima” (music by Yanada Tadashi). The song became a hit and made Jogashima famous as a place to visit.

SEE IT TODAYThe “Rain on Jogashima” poem stone (1949, in his handwriting) in the Hakushu monument garden under Jogashima Bridge; the Hakushu Memorial Hall nearby (10:00–16:00, closed Mon, Fri and over New Year); and a poem stone in his own hand at Kentoji Temple in Shiraishi.

ComposerRain on Jogashima

Yanada Tadashi1885–1959 · composer and teacher

Born in Sapporo, he studied at the Tokyo Music School and taught for many years at Tokyo’s First Prefectural Middle School. He set Hakushu’s “Rain on Jogashima” to music and also composed the children’s song “Donguri Korokoro”.

WarlordAburatsubo

Miura Dosun (Yoshiatsu)?–1516 · father of the last Miura lord

In 1516 Arai Castle at Aburatsubo fell to Hojo Soun’s army, and he is said to have died there with his son Yoshioki, ending the Miura clan after some 450 years. The war chronicle Hojo Godaiki records his farewell poem.

SEE IT TODAYHis grave at Aburatsubo, close to the Arai Castle site.
